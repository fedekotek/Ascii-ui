ascii/ui

shadcn-style components with a brutalist ASCII skin and a bad signal. Frames, fills and shadows are strings of characters, weight comes from how dense the character is, and every state change glitches a little. Native HTML underneath.

Long-press anything to break it. Press / or tap >_ for commands. Scroll fast and the signal tears. The footer is a game.

Rules

    

    
@@ %% ## ** ++ == :: ..

    
    
      
  • Weight is character density. @ is the loudest thing on the page and . is the quietest.
    • 
      
  • Frames, rules, fills and shadows are strings of characters. Hierarchy comes from which character.
    • 
      
  • Big type is a bitmap: every pixel of a headline is a character.
    • 
      
  • Color is signal: magenta acts, cyan focuses, lime confirms, yellow warns.
    • 
      
  • Glitch is feedback. It fires when you touch something or when a state changes, and the slider turns it down to zero.
    • 
      
  • Everything arrives broken and fixes itself: elements glitch in as they enter the screen, text decodes left to right.
    • 
      
  • Sound is feedback too. Square-wave blips and crushed noise, never music.
    • 
      
  • Nothing jumps. State changes travel through the ramp, one character at a time, at a low frame rate.
    • 
      
  • Underneath it is plain HTML: buttons, inputs, a dialog.
    •

Why

    

    
Four ideas hold the whole thing together.

    
Weight is density
Hierarchy comes from which character draws the frame. @ shouts, = talks and . whispers.

    
Frames are strings
Every border, rule and shadow is a run of characters clipped to the box. No images and no SVG.

    
Glitch is feedback
Tearing, RGB split and shards fire when you touch something or a state changes. One slider turns it all down.

    
HTML underneath
Real buttons, inputs, dialog and details. Keyboards and screen readers get the plain version.

  


  

    
How it is made

    

    
Five small labs. Each one is a piece of the system you can poke.

The glitch engine

    
Four effects, fired one at a time. On the rest of the page they fire on their own when state changes.

    

      
      
      
      
    


    
Sound is oscillators

    
No audio files. Square waves for blips, a sawtooth for errors and filtered noise for the glitches.

    

      
      
      
      
    

  


  

    
Questions

    

    

      
Is this accessible?
The characters are paint. Underneath are native controls with labels, focus states and keyboard support, and decorative glyphs are hidden from assistive tech. Reduced motion switches the glitch off.

      
Does it need a framework?
No. It is one HTML file. The components are classes and custom properties, so they drop into anything.

      
Why monospace only?
The grid is the design. Every width is a number of characters and every height a number of rows, which is what lets strings work as borders.

      
Can I turn the glitch off?
Yes. The Glitch switch kills it, the slider scales it, and the page respects reduced motion.
